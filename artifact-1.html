Analysis of Blanco and Hughes’ poems

Richard Blanco

This is an analysis of the poems and their meaning from writers Blanco and Hughes; both poems use rivers to convey emotions and cultural ideas. My goal is to dive into the deeper meaning of these poems the similarities between the both of them in; themes, ideas, and characterization.

Complaint of El Rio Grandits’s poem written by Richard Blanco symbolizes how a river was meant for all things to connect to the earth through the river itself, the river speaks of peace and being one with the earth and sea, it speaks of feeling the sun and watching the stars around the moon, long before humankind could speak, long before humankind tainted the river. Then humanity used the river as a way to divide themselves from each other and their land, they used the river as a border and built a giant wall. many people died trying to get across it, they took the peace and beauty the river once had and turned it into a capital of death and sorrow for migrants. The river that existed to connect all things is now the river that divides us. I believe the river symbolizes purity and connection, that was tarnished by governments and forced to be a river that divides people, places and things.

Blanco uses mental imagery through wording to set the general setting and develop the symbolisms he wants to get across, such as “You named me big river, drew me—blue,

thick to divide” (line 25) “I wasn’t meant to drown children, hear mother’s cries, never meant to be your geography: a line, a border” (line 15) with how descriptive and how much passion is in the wording, it helps the reader see and understand through these descriptive sentences. “Blue, thick to divide” shows the forced separation from one place to another, from one group across the map to their group across the map. Blanco also uses the title of the poem itself to make you understand that the river is speaking and it is frustrating.

Langston Hughes, The Negro Speaks of rivers.

This poem speaks of 4 rivers, the Euphrates River, the Congo river, the Nile River, Mississippi river, all these rivers share connections too African American history. Euphrates river is one of the cradles of ancient humanity and African ancestral roots, the Congo river is the heart of the African continent, reflecting the riches of African history, Nile River is a symbol of ancient Egypt, highlighting, survival and cultural memory across millennia.

Mississippi river, flowing through the us the Mississippi links African Americans to more recent history like slavery, resilience, and over all struggles. I think Hughes is using these ancient rivers as witnesses to all African American history from ancient times to current time’s “I bathed in the Euphrates when Dawans were young.” (line 5) And “I looked upon the Nile and raised the pyramids above it.” (line 5) speaks values on how these rivers have truly been there since the begging of African American culture. “I hear the singing of the Mississippi when Abe Lincoln went down to New Orleans, and I’ve seen its muddy bosom turn golden in the sunset” (5) this is the final river that has witnessed the last major struggle of African American history. Langston Hughes poem is short, but it speaks many words in the lack of contact it gives. Hughes makes the reader understand through the deeper meaning in his words he makes you think about real African history through these rives, you can truly imagine the time periods he speaks of when you read his wording and the rivers associated with it. as an African American Hughes is speaking of Africa history through these rivers saying how our people know these rivers very well.

The symbolism between these two poems

Blancos poem speaks more in the prospective of the river itself and it describes to the reader how it was made to unity everything and how in its nature it is a beautiful part of the earth. Then Blanco shifts to how people and governments came and turned the river into a borderline to divide people mainly immigrants that are trying to get a better life, people that are worse off on one side than the other because lines like these are drawn in the first place, here is there and there is here. Blanco speaks from the place of an immigrant, and this poem inspires the reader to truly think from his prospective through the guides of a river. Hughes does not speak of one river he speaks of many rivers; he links them to African American history and culture both poems share the prospective of people suffer from discrimination, division, and suffering by using these rivers as witnesses to it all and to tell these stories. Both poems use real world rivers that are not only major geographical divides between people but are also important to the writer’s culture and heritage.

Although Blanco and Hughes both use rivers to speak of division certain ethnic backgrounds, Blanco presents the river as its own entity that is tired of the role forced upon I, whereas Hughes presents the rivers as witnesses that were there for all the occurs of African American history, he speaks about the river as if they are good friends that have always been there.

Part B

How I identify with Blancos poem

I do identify with Blanco’s symbolism in complaint of el Rio grande. He speaks through the river, in the poem speaks as a being that is interpreted, used, and assigned cruel and unjust meaning and identity by others- when the river wants to be known as a great force that brings people together others don’t consider what the river was truly meant for and has always been. They see the river and assign their view of what it is and what it should be, they use concepts as borders and lines when in reality these are only human concepts and land, rivers, places, are not technically divided in the ways that we draw. I personally relate to this because others can easily assign values and ideas to you as a person without your input or trying to understand what you want and what you want to be viewed as and known for.

How I identify with Hughes poem

I do identify with the symbolism in Hughes’s poem, the negro speaks of rivers: his poem is very short but it does tell a great tail in its context and deeper meaning, he speaks of multiple rivers and he speaks as this ancient entity, (that being the race of Africa Americans) he repeats the same two lines in this poem, “my soul has grown deep like the rivers” (line 0 and line 10) I believe he is speaking of how the history of African Americans has grown deep and rich in culture as time went on just like the rivers he speaks of. I relate to the symbolism of attaching your growth along with a place or item, like how I was in Richmond all my life and now for the first time in a new chapter of my life I have left home to a new location. Just as how he speaks of the rivers, I also feel that I have grown alongside Richmond and Richmond has grown with me.

My river

a river I am familiar with is the James River located in the city Richmond Virgina, the James River has seen the many eras and phases of the city, as long as the river has flown with its vitality and sustainability so has the city with it. The river has seen all of Richmond, it saw the first settlers, it saw the houses being built then later the city. Just as the city kept developing the river kept flowing, the river represents resilience always flowing and never changing its perseverance is a reflection of the very first settlers that came here. No matter what happened around it, war, development, people coming and going, the river stayed steady. It kept flowing, kept pushing on and never lost its identity.

To me, the James River symbolizes how something can go through centuries of change and still holds on to its purpose and identity. It reminds me of how people grow too and can go through hardships and still be the same Just like the river, I’ve seen. The river’s endurance feels like a reflection of the first settlers who came here and expanded from single homes to small towns to the city it is today. but at the same time, it also feels like a reflection of my own life, always moving, always adapting, but still staying true to who I am and what my values are. The James River helps me express my experiences because its resilience matches my own always flowing, always growing, and never losing its strength. It’s an analogy for how I’ve moved through life, steady, persistent, and shaped, but not changed by everything I’ve witnessed.

This has been my thesis on Blanco and Hughes poems and my reflection on a river that holds meaning to me. diving into the meaning of these poems of rivers helped me see the deeper meaning that my river holds back home and the many challenge’s it and the people around it faced.
