Central Learning Claim

This semester I learned that having a quotation or example is not enough by itself. I have to explain what it means and how it connects to what I am trying to say. I also learned that there can be another way to understand the same evidence, so I need to explain why my interpretation makes sense.

Synthesis

In my first analysis of Blanco and Hughes, I noticed that both poets use rivers to speak about culture, history, and division. I explained how Blanco makes the river seem like a character that is frustrated with what people have made it into, while Hughes uses rivers as witnesses to African American history. I had ideas about what the poems meant, but sometimes I would put in a quotation and move on without explaining enough about the words themselves.

In Artifact 2, I started looking closer at how I was explaining my ideas. One example is the sentence about Blanco's river. My first version said the river was made to unify everything, but I did not really explain what the river was like before people turned it into a border. I added that it is beautiful in its nature and that helped explain what was changed. I also chose the words “thick to divide” and explained why I think the river is frustrated. I even looked at another possible interpretation where the river could just be observing what happened. I still believed my interpretation made more sense because of the title and the emotional wording in the poem.

I used some of these same ideas in my companion essay about battery replacement. I explained why companies should offer one free battery or essential component replacement during a device's lifespan. I also included the other side of the argument, which is that this could cost companies too much money. I did not want to ignore that point, so I explained how it could help customer satisfaction and gave another option of adding the replacement to a protection plan.

One thing I noticed across these pieces is how people can give something a meaning that does not really match what it is. In Blanco's poem, the river was meant to connect things, but people turned it into a border. I connected that to how people can assign values and ideas to you without understanding what you want to be known for. In my battery essay, people might think a whole device is outdated when the battery could be the main problem. These examples are different, but they both made me think about looking closer before deciding what something means.

I still have things I need to work on. Some of my writing has grammar mistakes, and I need to make sure my quotations and line numbers match the original poems. I want to get better at explaining my evidence instead of assuming the reader will understand what I mean. I also need to check my sources more carefully because the details matter when I am trying to prove a point.
