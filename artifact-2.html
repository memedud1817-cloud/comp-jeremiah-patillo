Tracing my interpretation

“Although Blanco and Hughes both use rivers to speak of division certain ethnic backgrounds, Blanco presents the river as its own entity that is tired of the role forced upon it, whereas Hughes presents the rivers as witnesses that were there for all the occurs of African American history.”

What I first noticed in both poems was how much gusto and strength was put into how the rivers “spoke” in Blanco’s poem, the river expresses frustration of being turned into a border and watching people get hurt trying to cross it “You named me big river… drew me-blue, thick to divide,” (line25) “but I wasn’t meant to drown children, hear mother’s cry’s” (line 25) the way the river speaks made me see it as a character with the capacity to think, feel, and express emotions. In Hughes poem, I took note of how the rivers acts as an ancient witness to major events in African American history, I really started to notice this trend when he writes “I bathed in the Euphrates when dawns were young.” (line 5)

“I heard the singing of the Mississippi when Lincoln went down to New Orleans” (line 5) these two lines show a start and an end section; these observations were put it into my final claim of how each poet uses rivers differently to express different but similar idea’s, my understanding changed from understanding the symbolisms in both poems to understanding how each poet uses rivers to represent cultural experiences.

Explaining a specific writing choice

Draft version

“Blancos poem speaks more in the prospective of the river itself and it describes to the reader how it was made to unify everything”

Final version

“Blancos poem speaks more in the prospective of the river itself and it describes to the reader how it was made to unify everything and how in its nature it is a beautiful part of the earth.”

In the earlier version, the sentence was unclear and bland because it didn’t fully explain what Blancos river was meant for and what it first represented before people came and changed it. The first version seemed to lack the emotional and symbolic weight of the river, that’s why in the final version, I added the wording “and how in its nature it is a beautiful part of the earth” to clarify the river’s original aspects and natural identity. The revision I added improved my literary analysis by making the symbolism more specific and helping the reader understand why Blanco’s river feels frustrated and misused. It truly adds to the reader’s understanding of what the river’s natural being was and why it is so frustrated after it was turned into a border.

Defending my analysis

“You named me big river, drew me-blue, thick to divide” (line 25)

I selected this quotation because it captures the core conflict in Blanco’s poem, that being the river’s natural identity verses the identity forced upon it by humanity. The key word “divide” is very essential to under to understanding the poem’s symbolism and greater ideas. in its original context, the river is speaking directly to humanity, explaining that it was never meant to be divider or a political tool. The phrase “drew-me blue, thick to divide” shows how humanity drew a line on a map and assigned the river a purpose it was never meant for or chose, maps, areas and places being separate is all a human concept that the river does not care for. The river’s frustration with its assigned role becomes clear through this mental image, and it reveals how human decisions can distort natural spaces and the people that live in them.

This quotation supports my thesis due to my claim arguing that Blanco presents the river as an entity that is tired of the role un-needlingly given to it. The river’s complaint in this phrase gives sight to how irritated it is, and how it is verbally fighting back against the identity humanity has imposed on it. The river wants to be known as a symbol of connection, beauty, and peace. But instead, it was forced to be a symbol of separation, danger, and political conflicts. This tension and idea are central to Blanco’s message about immigration and borders.

A reasonable alternative interpretation might argue that the river is not frustrated but simply observing how humanity has changed its meaning over time. Someone could say the river it neutral in it’s speaking and holds no emotions, that it is only describing what happened, they could interpret “thick to divide” as a factual statement rather than a complaint. However, I prefer my interpretation, and I believe it to be true due to the name of the poem: compliment of EL Rio grande. This sets the whole mood/tone of the poem just from the tittle, and the wording shows high emotions an example of this is “I wasn’t meant to drown children, hear mother’s cries” which clearly expresses sorrow and rebellion against the assigned role. This emotional language supports the idea that the river feels misused, frustrated, and bitter rather than being nonchalant about it all.

In total, this line highlights my understanding of Blanco’s symbolism. It shows how the river’s identity has been reshaped by human politics and ideas and how that said reshaping has and still continues to effect real people, especially immigrants who suffer due to river being turned into a dangerous barrier. my interpretation of Blanco’s writings highlights the poem’s emotional, political, and symbolic depth, making the quotation a strong foundation for my Analysis.
